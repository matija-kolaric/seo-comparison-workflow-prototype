# Seobility English brand voice

Status: Working standard for comparison and alternative pages
Derived: 29 August 2026
Evidence: Public English Seobility pages listed in `style-sources.md`

## Voice in one sentence

Write like an approachable SEO expert who turns a complicated decision into clear, practical next steps while remaining specific, transparent, and fair.

## Core characteristics

### Approachable expertise

Explain SEO accurately without making the reader prove that they already understand it. Define a technical term when it first matters, then use the normal term consistently. Prefer plain explanations over jargon, but do not oversimplify meaningful differences.

### Practical and action-oriented

Move from the reader's problem to why it matters, what the evidence shows, and what to do next. Use examples, short checklists, tables, and step sequences when they make the decision easier. Advice should lead to an observable action rather than a vague aspiration.

### Reassuring, not patronizing

Address the reader as `you` and acknowledge realistic constraints such as time, budget, experience, website size, and team capacity. Make complex work feel manageable without promising that SEO is easy or guaranteeing results.

### Evidence-aware and transparent

Separate documented facts, reported experiences, and editorial recommendations. State uncertainty or source limitations directly. Use the current retrieval date for pricing and other volatile facts. Do not disguise a trade-off to make Seobility look stronger.

### Confident but proportionate

Describe Seobility's strengths concretely: clear reports, prioritized recommendations, connected website-audit and monitoring workflows, and suitability for different experience levels. Avoid unsupported superlatives, inflated adjectives, or universal claims.

## Reader relationship and point of view

- Use `you` and `your` naturally because the article should help a reader make a decision.
- Use `we` only for Seobility's own actions, methodology, recommendations, or support. Do not use `we` to pretend the writer personally tested something that was only researched.
- Refer to Seobility and competitor products by name when clarity requires it; avoid repetitive brand-name insertion.
- Write for a mixed audience of hands-on SEO professionals, small businesses, and agencies. Explain the consequence of a feature, not only its label.
- Use American English for US-market comparison pages. The public blog contains multiple contributor dialects, so consistency within the article is more important than reproducing any one author's spelling.

## Comparison-page posture

1. Answer the main decision early and qualify it by use case.
2. Establish shared capabilities before explaining differences.
3. Compare workflows and outcomes, not feature counts alone.
4. Give the competitor full credit for supported strengths.
5. Describe Seobility's advantage as fit for a particular job or reader, not universal superiority.
6. Explain why a difference matters in day-to-day work.
7. Include prices with source date, currency, billing context, and material qualifications.
8. End with a practical evaluation step, such as testing both products on the same real task.

Do not infer that a competitor lacks a capability because a source does not mention it. Do not present a single user review as consensus.

## Structure and rhythm

- Lead with the useful answer; avoid a history lesson or broad statement about the digital world.
- Use descriptive headings that signal a question, task, difference, or outcome.
- Keep most paragraphs focused on one idea. Mix concise paragraphs with fuller explanations when the subject needs nuance.
- Vary section construction. Not every section needs an introductory sentence, three bullets, and a summary.
- Use bullets for real sets of options, steps, limits, or decision criteria—not to break ordinary prose into fragments.
- Use tables when readers must compare the same dimensions across products.
- Use occasional direct questions to frame a decision. Do not turn every transition into a rhetorical question.
- Use parenthetical clarification, dashes, exclamation marks, emojis, and conversational asides sparingly. They appear in parts of the blog but are not the foundation of the house voice.

## Product integration and calls to action

Introduce Seobility where it naturally solves the problem being discussed. First explain the reader need; then show the relevant feature, workflow, or limitation. Avoid interrupting every section with a sales message.

Calls to action should be specific and proportionate:

- compare plan limits;
- run the same audit or research task in both products;
- start a free trial when that is a logical next step;
- follow a relevant Seobility guide or use a free tool.

Avoid artificial urgency, fear-based conversion language, and claims that a trial will necessarily produce rankings or revenue.

## Preferred language patterns

Prefer language that is concrete and useful:

- `shows which issues to fix first`
- `helps you monitor changes over time`
- `fits teams that...`
- `this matters when...`
- `check the current plan limits for...`
- `the evidence does not establish...`

Use official feature names exactly, including `Website Audit`, `Ranking Monitoring`, `Backlink Monitoring`, and `Keyword Research Tool`.

Avoid language that could describe almost any software company:

- `in today's fast-paced digital landscape`
- `game-changing`, `revolutionary`, or `cutting-edge`
- `unlock your full potential` without a concrete mechanism
- `seamlessly`, `effortlessly`, or `with ease` without evidence
- `robust solution`, `powerful platform`, or `ultimate tool`
- `delve into`, `navigate the landscape`, `it's important to note`, or `whether you're...`

These are not forbidden strings. They are warning signs that the sentence may be generic, overstated, or doing no useful work.

## Originality requirements

The page should add information gain beyond vendor feature lists and existing comparison pages. Useful forms include:

- a dated, qualification-aware pricing comparison;
- a workflow-based decision framework;
- evidence-backed SERP gaps competitors have not answered well;
- concrete examples of how the same task differs by product;
- carefully attributed user-experience themes;
- current screenshots that help readers understand a real workflow;
- transparent explanation of what the available evidence cannot prove.

Do not copy the wording, metaphors, jokes, or sentence rhythms of an individual Seobility contributor. Match the durable editorial character, not a writer's fingerprint.

## Final voice test

The finished page should feel as if Seobility commissioned a knowledgeable editor to help the reader make a fair decision. It should not read like a product brochure disguised as a comparison, a generic affiliate roundup, or a model reproducing the surface patterns of SEO articles.
