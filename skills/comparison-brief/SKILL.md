# Comparison Brief

Read `input.json`, `research/research.md`, `research/serp-analysis.md`, `research/claims.json`, and `knowledge/seobility/citation-policy.md`. Verify the research handoff gate before writing `brief.md`. If it fails, stop with a concise list of missing research rather than filling gaps from memory.

The brief must define:

- audience and search intent;
- primary query and a small set of natural secondary terms;
- priority clusters and their intent;
- page angle and reader outcome;
- specific information gain supported by gap IDs;
- comparison dimensions justified by the research;
- proposed title, description, and outline;
- supported claim IDs available to each section;
- an image plan stating purpose, preferred source, evidence/caption need, and whether the asset is required or optional;
- a selective citation plan mapping volatile facts, exact figures, sensitive comparisons, review evidence, and other important claims to a descriptive link, numbered footnote, screenshot attribution, or internal trace only;
- important uncertainties and claims to avoid;
- a useful, non-manipulative next step for the reader.

Every major section must map to at least one supported claim, gap ID, priority query, or documented reader need. Do not add product facts, silently upgrade a claim status, or force sections for which the research has no useful evidence. Prefer a shorter evidence-backed outline over a comprehensive but generic one.

Keep the brief human-readable Markdown. It is a writing plan, not another data model.

Finish with a handoff checklist confirming that all major sections map to research, the information gain is explicit, unsupported topics are excluded, every proposed visual has a purpose and preferred source, and claims requiring visible verification have a planned citation treatment. Do not pass the brief when any required item fails.
